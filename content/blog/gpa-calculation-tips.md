# Calculate Your GPA Accurately

Your Grade Point Average (GPA) is a critical component of your academic record. Whether you're applying to colleges, scholarships, or graduate programs, understanding how GPA is calculated helps you track progress and set realistic goals.

## What is GPA?

GPA measures overall academic performance on a scale from 0.0 to 4.0 (in most US schools). Each letter grade corresponds to a numerical value: A=4.0, B=3.0, C=2.0, D=1.0, F=0.0. Your GPA reflects the average quality of work across all completed courses.

## How GPA is Calculated

The basic formula involves multiplying each course's grade value by its credit hours, summing all results, then dividing by total credit hours attempted. This weighted calculation means courses with more credits have greater impact on your final GPA.

**Example**: If you took a 3-credit A course (4.0 × 3 = 12.0) and a 4-credit B course (3.0 × 4 = 12.0), your GPA would be 24.0 ÷ 7 = 3.43

## Types of GPA

**Cumulative GPA**: Your overall average across all semesters taken
**Semester GPA**: Your average for a single semester only
**Major GPA**: Average for courses related to your major only
**Weighted vs. Unweighted**: Some schools weight honors or AP courses differently

## Factors Affecting Your GPA

- Course difficulty and credit hours
- Retaken courses (some schools use latest attempt, others average all attempts)
- Pass/fail courses (often don't affect GPA)
- Transfer courses (might not count toward GPA)
- Incomplete grades and withdrawals

## Tips for Improving Your GPA

1. **Prioritize challenging courses early**: Build strong foundation
2. **Attend classes consistently**: Attendance correlates with better grades
3. **Seek help early**: Visit office hours before struggling
4. **Form study groups**: Collaborative learning improves retention
5. **Use available resources**: Tutoring centers, writing labs, peer mentors
