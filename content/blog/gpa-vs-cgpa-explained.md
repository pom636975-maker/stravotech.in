Many students get tripped up by the difference between GPA and CGPA. While they sound similar, GPA (Grade Point Average) typically refers to performance in a single term, whereas CGPA (Cumulative GPA) aggregates performance over multiple terms or semesters. Knowing when colleges use GPA vs CGPA—especially for admissions and scholarships—can guide how you plan your coursework. This article demystifies both metrics, shows example calculations across 4.0 and 10.0 scales, and highlights how conversion between percentage-based systems and GPA works. We'll also cover institutional variations, such as how some universities treat pass/fail courses or replace earlier grades with later attempts. Actionable advice includes how to prioritize high-credit courses, when to retake classes, and how to use Stravotech calculators to model outcomes.
