## How to Convert Salary to Hourly Rate – The Complete Guide

Whether you are comparing a salaried job offer with a freelance hourly rate, trying to understand your true per-hour earnings, or negotiating a new contract, knowing how to convert an annual salary to an hourly wage is an essential financial skill.

This guide explains the formula, handles real-world scenarios like overtime and part-time work, and includes worked examples for both USD and INR salary structures.

## The Basic Salary to Hourly Formula

The standard conversion assumes a full-time work schedule:

Hourly Rate = Annual Salary ÷ (52 weeks × 40 hours per week)

Hourly Rate = Annual Salary ÷ 2,080

This is based on 52 weeks per year and the standard 40-hour work week used in the United States and many other countries.

Hourly Rate = $60,000 ÷ 2,080 = $28.85 per hour

## Worked Examples (INR – India)

For Indian salary packages, the calculation uses working days rather than weeks, because Indian work schedules typically have 250–260 working days per year (accounting for weekends and public holidays).

**Standard Indian Formula:**

Daily Rate = Annual CTC ÷ 250 working days

Hourly Rate = Daily Rate ÷ 8 working hours

**Example: ₹12,00,000 Annual CTC (₹12 Lakhs)**

Daily Rate = ₹12,00,000 ÷ 250 = ₹4,800 per day

Hourly Rate = ₹4,800 ÷ 8 = ₹600 per hour

**Example: ₹6,00,000 Annual CTC (₹6 Lakhs)**

Daily Rate = ₹6,00,000 ÷ 250 = ₹2,400 per day

Hourly Rate = ₹2,400 ÷ 8 = ₹300 per hour

## Breaking Salary into All Time Periods

Beyond just hourly rate, it's useful to know your daily, weekly, and monthly earnings from an annual salary.

Using $60,000 as an example:

Annual: $60,000

Monthly: $60,000 ÷ 12 = $5,000 per month

Weekly: $60,000 ÷ 52 = $1,153.85 per week

Daily: $60,000 ÷ 260 = $230.77 per day (based on 260 workdays)

Hourly: $60,000 ÷ 2,080 = $28.85 per hour

## Accounting for Vacations and Holidays

The standard 2,080-hour calculation does not account for paid time off. If you have vacation days and holidays, your effective working hours are fewer, meaning your "true" hourly rate is slightly higher.

**More Accurate Formula:**

Effective Hours = 2,080 – (Vacation Days + Holidays) × 8

Effective Hourly Rate = Annual Salary ÷ Effective Hours

**Example with 15 vacation days + 10 public holidays:**

Effective Hours = 2,080 – (15 + 10) × 8 = 2,080 – 200 = 1,880 hours

Effective Rate = $60,000 ÷ 1,880 = $31.91 per hour

This is your true effective hourly rate — the amount you actually earn per hour worked.

## Using This for Freelance Rate Setting

One of the most practical uses of salary-to-hourly conversion is setting freelance rates. If you want to earn the equivalent of a $70,000 full-time salary as a freelancer, you cannot simply charge $33.65/hour.

As a freelancer you need to cover:

**Self-Employment Taxes:** Freelancers pay both employee and employer portions of Social Security and Medicare taxes — approximately 15.3% on top of income tax.

**No Benefits:** No paid vacation, no health insurance, no retirement matching. You must fund these from your billing rate.

**Not All Hours Are Billable:** You will spend 20–30% of your time on administration, marketing, and unbillable tasks. Only 70–80% of your hours will be client-billable.

## The Freelance Rate Formula

To match a $70,000 salary as a freelancer:

Step 1: Add self-employment tax and benefits cost:
$70,000 × 1.20 (20% buffer) = $84,000 target gross

Step 2: Account for non-billable time:
Assuming 70% utilization: billable hours = 2,080 × 0.70 = 1,456 hours/year

Step 3: Calculate rate:
Required Rate = $84,000 ÷ 1,456 = $57.69 per hour

So to match a $70,000 salary, you should charge approximately $57–65 per hour as a freelancer.

## Gross vs Net (After-Tax) Hourly Rate

The rates calculated above are all gross (pre-tax) figures. Your actual take-home pay per hour will be lower after income tax, Social Security, Medicare, and any state/local taxes.

**For US workers (rough estimate):**

Effective tax rate depends heavily on income level and filing status. A single filer earning $60,000 might pay 18–22% effective rate overall.

After-tax hourly = $28.85 × (1 – 0.20) ≈ $23.08 per hour

## Salary vs Hourly: Which Is Better?

There is no universal answer — it depends on your situation:

**Salaried positions typically offer:**
- Stability and predictable income
- Benefits (health insurance, retirement plans, paid leave)
- Performance-based bonuses and career advancement paths
- Simpler tax filing

**Hourly positions typically offer:**
- Overtime pay for hours above 40/week (in the US, under FLSA)
- More flexibility
- More direct link between hours worked and pay received
- May or may not include benefits depending on employer and hours

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How many hours are in a work year?**
A: Standard: 2,080 hours (52 weeks × 40 hours). Subtracting 10 holidays: approximately 2,000 effective hours. Subtracting additional vacation: 1,880–1,960 hours.

**Q: Does the salary-to-hourly calculator show net or gross pay?**
A: All conversions show gross (pre-tax) pay. Your net (take-home) pay will be lower after income taxes and deductions.

**Q: What is $20 per hour annually?**
A: $20/hour × 2,080 hours = $41,600 per year gross.

**Q: What is $15 per hour annually?**
A: $15/hour × 2,080 = $31,200 per year gross (approximately the US federal minimum wage annualized).
