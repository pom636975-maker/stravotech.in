## Understanding Your Mortgage Payment: The Complete PITI Guide

Buying a home is one of the biggest financial decisions you will ever make. When you look at a listing and see an estimated monthly payment, it often consists of much more than just the loan repayment. To budget correctly, you need to understand **PITI**.

PITI stands for **Principal, Interest, Taxes, and Insurance**. This guide explains each component and how they impact your monthly mortgage calculator results.

## 1. Principal
The principal is the actual amount of money you borrowed to buy the home. For example, if you buy a house for $400,000 and put down $80,000, your beginning principal is $320,000. Each month, a portion of your payment goes toward paying down this balance.

## 2. Interest
Interest is the cost of borrowing that money. It is calculated as a percentage of your remaining principal balance. In the early stages of a 30-year mortgage, the majority of your monthly payment goes toward interest rather than the principal.

## 3. Taxes (Property Taxes)
Local governments charge property taxes to fund public services like schools, police, and road repairs. These taxes are typically calculated as a percentage of your home's assessed value. Even though you pay these to your mortgage lender each month, they place the money in an **escrow account** and pay the local government on your behalf once or twice a year.

## 4. Insurance
There are two main types of insurance that can be included in your mortgage payment:
- **Homeowners Insurance:** Protects your property from damage (fire, storms, theft). Lenders require this to protect their collateral.
- **Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI):** If you put down less than 20% on a conventional loan, you will likely have to pay PMI. This protects the lender if you default on the loan.

## How the Formula Works

The math behind a mortgage payment is a bit more complex than a standard loan because of the long tenure (usually 15 or 30 years).

**Monthly Principal and Interest (PI) = P [ i(1 + i)^n ] / [ (1 + i)^n – 1 ]**

Where:
- **P** = Principal loan amount
- **i** = Monthly interest rate (Annual rate / 12)
- **n** = Number of months (360 for a 30-year loan)

## Example: A $350,000 Home Purchase

If you buy a $350,000 home with a 20% down payment ($70,000) and a 7% interest rate on a 30-year fixed mortgage:

- **Loan Amount:** $280,000
- **Monthly Principal & Interest:** $1,863
- **Estimated Taxes & Insurance:** $450
- **Total Monthly PITI Payment:** **$2,313**

## Factors That Change Your Monthly Payment

**1. Interest Rates:** This is the most significant factor. A move from 6% to 7% on a $300k loan adds nearly $200 per month to your payment.

**2. Down Payment:** The more you pay upfront, the lower your principal balance and the lower your monthly payment. Additionally, a 20% down payment eliminates the need for PMI.

**3. Loan Tenure:**
- **30-Year Mortgage:** Lower monthly payments, but you pay much more in total interest over the life of the loan.
- **15-Year Mortgage:** Higher monthly payments, but you pay off the house in half the time and save tens of thousands in interest.

**4. Credit Score:** Lenders offer the best interest rates to those with scores above 740. A lower score can result in a higher interest rate, significantly increasing your monthly cost.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can I pay my own taxes and insurance?**
A: Some lenders allow you to pay your own taxes and insurance (waiving the escrow requirement) if you have at least 20% equity in the home. However, many homeowners find the escrow system more convenient.

**Q: Will my mortgage payment ever change?**
A: If you have a **Fixed-Rate Mortgage**, your principal and interest will never change. However, your total payment can change if your local property tax rates or insurance premiums go up or down.

**Q: Should I choose a 15-year or 30-year loan?**
A: Choose 15 years if you want to be debt-free faster and can afford the higher monthly payment. Choose 30 years if you want lower monthly payments and more flexibility in your monthly budget.
